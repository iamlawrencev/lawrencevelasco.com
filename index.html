About

I do both. Neither is the backup plan.

I started in 2016 as a software developer. A few years in, I also took on more responsibility for the project side — scope, timelines, talking to the people who actually wanted the thing built. I kept doing both rather than picking one. Projects tend to go sideways when someone is too focused on just the code or just the plan around it: either the build drifts from what people actually need, or the plan stops matching what's realistic to build. Being equally capable on both sides is what keeps a project moving smoothly, and it's the strength I bring to the table.

Developer’s perspective

I still write and review code regularly, so I have a current sense of architecture, platform limits, and the tradeoffs that come with maintainability and performance. When someone's excited about an idea, I can usually tell early on what's going to be simple and what's going to be a slog.

Manager’s perspective

On the other side, I'm the one thinking about scope, priorities, and what a client or team actually needs to hear about progress — including the parts nobody enjoys, like saying a deadline needs to move. Having both perspectives at once means fewer surprises for everyone by the time something ships.

FAQ

How I work

What do you do?

I write code and manage the projects it's part of. Being equally capable at both is the strength I bring — a lot of project problems come from someone being too focused on just one side or the other.

How do you split code and project work?

It depends on the project. Some weeks I'm mostly writing code, other weeks mostly managing scope and communication. I keep both active rather than letting one go quiet, since neither side works well without the other close by.

What technologies do you work with?

My core stack is React Native, .NET MAUI, native iOS and Android, TypeScript, C#, and .NET, plus databases, business systems, automation, and some AI-assisted development along the way.

What products have you shipped?

On my own time, I've shipped San Bidet (a restroom finder), AniVoice (an anime voice-actor lookup), and Pitchie (a pitch-recognition game).

Building something, or thinking about it?

Send me a note about what it is and where you're at. I'll get back to you and we can figure out the rest from there.
